# Learning site UX contract

Use this contract for every generated site. Adapt the visual expression to the report, but do not remove the interaction and readability guarantees below.

## Content-led direction

- Derive the visual brief from the report title, overview, article or dialogue type, chapter summaries, language mix, and sentence density.
- Make the interface feel appropriate to the source. For example, use an editorial reading-workbench direction for interviews or essays and a transcript-oriented direction for timed dialogue.
- Use report content for hierarchy and labels. Do not invent themes, claims, imagery, chapter names, or metadata.
- Avoid a generic dashboard composition when the source is primarily an article. Prioritize reading, then grammar inspection, then supporting metadata.
- Prefer quiet surfaces, restrained separators, and compact navigation over decorative grids, oversized empty areas, or an ornamental sidebar. Keep colors and type choices content-led rather than prescribing one fixed theme.

## Required reading loop

Keep this path obvious and functional:

1. Open the overview.
2. Start reading or enter through a chapter.
3. Locate the active chapter and sentence.
4. Select a grammar point and read its explanation.
5. Move to the previous or next sentence.
6. Return to the overview without losing theme or UI-language state.

Use shareable URL state for view, sentence, grammar point, filters, UI language, and theme when applicable.

## Information architecture and controls

- Keep search visible in the reading view.
- Keep secondary language and level filters available without letting them dominate the mobile viewport.
- Provide a clear-filter action and an accurate visible-result count.
- Keep sentences without grammar points out of every reading entry point, including overview segments, search, sentence index, previous/next navigation, and timeline. Counts and display positions describe only eligible learning sentences; clearing filters must not restore excluded sentences.
- Give chapter, sentence, grammar, and navigation controls distinct active, hover, focus, disabled, and pressed states.
- Keep previous and next controls labeled; do not rely on arrows alone.
- Provide a compact chapter/segment selector instead of consuming a column with a decorative route rail. Where segments are generated by equal sentence counts, label that method rather than implying editorial chapters.
- Provide a labeled sentence-number jump with a submit button. Use the full eligible sentence list's one-based display position, retain original IDs in the URL, and reject blank, fractional, or out-of-range values. Explain that jumping clears active filters; after a valid jump, update selection, grammar, position, and URL together.
- Support left/right arrow keys for previous/next eligible sentence within the current filters. Do not intercept arrow keys inside inputs, textareas, selects, editable content, or controls that use them, and do not override modified shortcuts. Keep on-screen navigation available and expose a short keyboard hint on desktop.
- Keep the active sentence visible in the sentence index. Center it automatically when the index scrolls horizontally.
- On narrow screens, scroll the selected grammar explanation into view.
- Provide a mobile “Back to sentence” action near the explanation. It must only return the viewport to the original sentence, preserving the selected grammar point and filters. Avoid combined scroll padding and scroll margins that leave excessive blank space or hide content behind fixed controls.
- Collapse long timelines by default. The timeline navigation link must open the section before moving to it; individual entries still select the sentence and synchronize its URL. Bound the expanded list's scrolling area.
- Do not render inactive overview or navigation controls that appear clickable but do nothing.

## Typography and reading comfort

- Prefer readable editorial line lengths over filling every available pixel.
- Use responsive sentence sizing. Reduce type progressively for long and very long sentences while keeping comfortable line height.
- Keep grammar explanations and essential details at normal reading size; reserve small mono text for coordinates and secondary metadata.
- Avoid all-caps body copy, dense unbroken paragraphs, and low-contrast essential text.
- Keep hierarchy visible through size, weight, spacing, and alignment rather than color alone.
- Place the translation directly after the original sentence, before secondary metadata. Show the reading once in a keyboard-accessible disclosure by default, with an explicit missing-reading message when expanded; do not repeat the same reading above and below the sentence.
- Treat original sentences as reading text, not oversized display headlines. A useful starting range is 26–32 px on desktop and 22–25 px on phones, reducing toward 20 px for dense mobile passages with generous line height. Adapt to content, available fonts, and user zoom rather than enforcing these as fixed sizes.
- Make grammar choices compact, legible controls with a clear active state and useful level labels. Allow wrapping on phones so multiple grammar points are discoverable without a long horizontal chip strip.

## Responsive behavior

At desktop widths, use a balanced two-column reading area with a constrained overall width. Keep the sentence and grammar inspector simultaneously usable without making the explanation column too narrow. Prefer normal page scrolling to nested independently scrolling reading panels; collapse to one column before either side becomes cramped.

At mobile widths:

- Keep the primary search field visible and collapse secondary filters behind an explicit expanded-state button.
- Provide a fixed bottom reading dock for previous sentence, overview or position, and next sentence.
- Add enough bottom padding that the dock never covers the final content.
- Use at least 44 px targets for primary touch controls and at least 40 px for compact sentence-index controls.
- Prevent horizontal page overflow. Allow deliberate horizontal scrolling only inside bounded index or chip regions.
- Keep the first meaningful sentence content within roughly the first 45% of a typical phone viewport when filters are collapsed.
- Respect safe-area insets and reduced-motion preferences.

## Accessibility

- Use semantic landmarks, headings, lists, labels, and native controls.
- Expose current chapter or sentence with `aria-current` and selected grammar with `aria-pressed` or equivalent semantics.
- Maintain visible keyboard focus and logical tab order.
- Preserve a skip link to main content.
- Do not communicate state through color alone.

## Browser acceptance

Run the full matrix below for a new site, release acceptance, or changes to shared navigation, layout, or state. For a local text, style, or component change, check the affected page, language, viewport, and interaction plus relevant regressions; expand coverage when the affected behavior is shared. Documentation-only and packaging tasks do not require browser QA. The UX guarantees above remain applicable regardless of validation scope.

For full acceptance, verify at minimum at 1440×900 and 390×844:

- No horizontal page overflow.
- Overview, start-reading, chapter, sentence, grammar, previous, next, clear-filter, filter-toggle, and return-to-overview controls work.
- Sentence-number jump reaches the intended display position, clears filters as explained, and rejects invalid values without changing selection.
- Arrow-key navigation respects current filters and list boundaries; typing and moving the caret in search/number inputs does not change sentences.
- Reading disclosure expands/collapses by pointer and keyboard without duplicating the reading. The timeline starts collapsed, opens from its navigation link, and supports sentence selection and collapse.
- Mobile grammar selection reveals the explanation; “Back to sentence” preserves that grammar selection and returns the original to view.
- Mobile filter expansion and collapse update both the UI and accessibility state.
- The fixed mobile dock remains usable while content scrolls.
- Long sentences remain readable without clipping.
- Active sentence and grammar state agree with the URL.
- Light, dark, and system themes remain usable.
- Chinese, Japanese, and English UI modes do not break layout.
- Browser console contains no errors or unexpected warnings.
- Production assets, favicon, and immutable report data load successfully.

For full acceptance, capture desktop and mobile screenshots after the interaction checks. For targeted visual changes, capture the affected view when useful to verify the change. Report the checks performed and any relevant browser or platform coverage not exercised; do not present targeted checks as full-site acceptance.
